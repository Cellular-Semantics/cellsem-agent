 The authors declare no competing interests. 01 3 2020 10 2 2020 21 3 362414 153 168 30 06 2020 01 09 2020 01 09 2020 31 07 2020 16 06 2024 The dentate gyrus (DG) has a key role in hippocampal memory formation. Intriguingly, DG lesions impair many, but not all hippocampus-dependent mnemonic functions, indicating that the rest of the hippocampus (CA1-CA3) can operate autonomously under certain conditions. An extensive body of theoretical work has proposed how the architectural elements and various cell types of the DG may underlie its function in cognition. Recent studies recorded and manipulated the activity of different neuron types in the DG during memory tasks and opened exciting new insights in the mechanisms of DG computational processes, particularly for encoding, retrieval and discrimination of similar memories.
